Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Perth Amboy Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

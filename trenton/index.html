Restoration Challenges Trenton Homeowners Face

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Trenton property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. On Trenton roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Perth Amboy Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

Preventing Water Damage in Trenton Homes

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Trenton homeowner.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Next come appliances. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Homeowners in Trenton can ask Perth Amboy Restoration about any of this. If something does fail, calling quickly is what keeps the damage contained.

What to Do Before Our Trenton Crew Arrives

The right first steps change with the type of water on your floor. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Trenton crew start faster. Clear a path from the door to the wet area and move cars so the truck can park close. Write down when you found the water and what you think caused it. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Do not assume clean water will remain that way. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Trenton leak seems small.

What Policies Usually Cover

In general, a standard homeowner's policy pays for water damage when the event was accidental and happened suddenly. This includes burst pipes, appliance failures (washing machines, water heaters, dishwashers), ice dam damage, accidental overflow from sinks or bathtubs, and sudden HVAC system failures. If you cannot stay in the house, temporary living expenses are typically included, as are water removal, structural dry-out, replacement materials, and the remaining restoration costs.

Damage That Is Often Excluded

The usual exclusions are sewage backup unless you carry a specific endorsement, outside flooding unless you hold separate flood insurance, groundwater seeping through the foundation, slow leaks and other gradual damage, and anything traced to neglected or deferred maintenance. The time to review exclusions is before anything happens, while you can still buy extra policies or endorsements to cover what is missing.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

A few steps protect your claim after a water loss, the first being video and photos of all damage before any cleanup. Contact your insurance company promptly to report the loss. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Do not throw away anything that was damaged before the adjuster sees it. Get professional restoration from an IICRC-certified company. Save every receipt tied to the loss, from temporary repairs to living expenses. When the adjuster visits, be there to walk them through all of the damage.
